Notification settings decide where TheOptimizer reaches you when something happens: a rule fires, an ad account stops syncing, or the platform spots something worth acting on. You configure them under **Settings → Notifications**.

{/* TODO: screenshot — Settings page, Notifications tab, showing the Email / Slack / Telegram channel cards */}

Alerts are delivered to **destinations**, and destinations are grouped by **channel**. Each channel holds several destinations — five on most accounts — so you can send rule alerts to your own inbox, account problems to a shared Slack channel, and everything to your phone over Telegram — all at the same time.

## The three alert types

Every destination has its own alert selection. These are the alerts you can choose from:

| Alert | When it fires |
| --- | --- |
| **Rule Executed** | Every time one of your automation rules runs and takes an action |
| **Account Issues** | Wrong API info, login problems, or missing tracking tokens on a connected account |
| **Smart Notifications** | Periodic optimization suggestions and performance alerts from TheOptimizer |

<Tip>
  A destination with no alerts selected receives nothing. If a teammate reports that Slack has gone quiet, check the alert selection on that destination before looking anywhere else.
</Tip>

## Connect a destination

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the wizard">
    Go to **Settings → Notifications**. With nothing connected yet, the page shows a **Never miss an alert** screen with a tile for each channel — click the one you want. Otherwise use the **Add** button on the channel card you want to extend, or **Add Email** / **Add Slack** / **Add Telegram** on a card that is still empty.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Choose the channel">
    Pick **Email**, **Slack**, or **Telegram**. A channel that has used all of its slots shows **Limit reached** and cannot be selected until you remove a destination.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Configure it">
    What you fill in depends on the channel — see the tabs below.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Select the alerts">
    Under **Send these alerts here**, tick the alerts this destination should receive, then click **Add destination**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Email">
    Enter a **Name** to identify the destination later (for example, `On-call inbox`) and the **Email address** that should receive the alerts.

    The address does not have to be your login email — a shared team inbox or an alias works just as well.

    {/* TODO: screenshot — Add destination wizard, email step with Name and Email address fields */}
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Slack">
    Click **Authorize Slack**. Slack asks you to pick the workspace and the channel TheOptimizer may post into, then returns you to the app.

    Once authorization completes, the wizard confirms the workspace and channel it connected to and asks you to **Name this connection** — `#alerts` or `#media-buying` makes the destination easy to recognize in the list.

    <Note>
      TheOptimizer posts into the channel you select during authorization. To send alerts to a second Slack channel, add another Slack destination and authorize again, choosing the other channel.
    </Note>

    {/* TODO: screenshot — Add destination wizard, Slack step with the Authorize Slack button */}
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Telegram">
    Click the Telegram login button and authorize TheOptimizer to send you messages. Telegram confirms the chat, and the wizard shows the linked account.

    Because Telegram links to a personal chat, each team member connects their own — there is nothing to share or forward.

    {/* TODO: screenshot — Add destination wizard, Telegram step with the authorize button */}
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

<Note>
  The same email address, Slack channel, or Telegram chat cannot be added twice within a channel. If a destination already exists, adjust its alert selection instead of adding a duplicate.
</Note>

## Manage existing destinations

Each channel card lists its destinations and shows how many of the allowed slots are in use — for example, `2/5 connected`.

Every destination row offers:

| Control | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Toggle** | Turns notifications on or off for that destination without removing it — useful for pausing alerts while you are away |
| **Manage alerts** | Expands the row so you can change which of the three alerts are delivered there |
| **Remove destination** | Deletes the destination. It stops receiving alerts immediately, and you can connect it again later |

{/* TODO: screenshot — a channel card with one destination row expanded, showing the three alert checkboxes */}

Changes save as you make them — there is no separate save step.

<Tip>
  Pausing a destination is almost always better than removing it. The toggle keeps the alert selection intact, so switching alerts back on is a single click instead of a trip back through the wizard.
</Tip>

## A setup that works well

If you run rules across several ad accounts, this split keeps the noise manageable:

- **Rule Executed** → Slack, in a channel the whole team watches. Rule activity is the highest-volume alert and reads best as a running feed.
- **Account Issues** → email, to whoever owns the integrations. These need someone to actually fix something, so they deserve an inbox.
- **Smart Notifications** → Telegram, on your own phone. They are suggestions, not emergencies, and they are useful to skim.
